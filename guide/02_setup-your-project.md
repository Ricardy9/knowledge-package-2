# 02 Set up your project

**Who:** you answer, the AI guide builds. **Command:** `/setup`. **Time:** about 15 minutes.

Type `/setup`. The AI guide asks these questions one at a time. The example answers come from the Mexico example.

| Question | Example answer |
|---|---|
| A short name for the project | `mexico-displacement` |
| What is it about? | Forced internal displacement in Mexico: its scale, causes, the most affected states, and the response. |
| What period? | 2018–2025 |
| Who will read the results, and what will they decide? | Researchers and policy staff preparing a briefing; where support and further research should focus. |
| Your 3–5 main questions | How large is displacement and how is it changing? What drives it? Who and where is most affected? What is the legal and policy response? |
| Themes to group your sources | scale-and-trends, causes, affected-groups-and-places, policy-and-response |
| Do you have documents already? | Yes, or "help me find a starter list" |

## What the AI guide then does
1. Creates your project folder: `projects/<your project name>/`.
2. Writes your answers into `01_project-brief/project_profile.md`.
3. Creates one folder per theme inside `02_sources/`.
4. Splits each main question into small questions, each asking about **one** checkable thing. For example, "How large is displacement?" becomes "How many new conflict- and violence-related internal displacements were recorded in Mexico in 2024?"
5. Shows you the list. **You** can add, remove or reword questions before they are saved.
6. Saves them to `03_questions/question_bank.csv` and gives you a checklist.

## Why small questions?
A question that asks about several things at once usually gets a good answer about one of them, while the others quietly disappear. One question per thing means every item gets its own answer, or its own "not found".
